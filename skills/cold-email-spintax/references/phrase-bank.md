# Phrase bank: safe swaps, context rules and the never-swap list

Derived from the GROUPS and CONTEXT tables in the rule-based engine, then tightened where the engine's output showed a weak swap. Every option in a group fits the same grammatical slot. A swap is only safe when it also keeps the meaning in this particular sentence, so read the context rules before using a group.

Use the groups as a starting point, not a ceiling. A model may add a swap that is not listed if it passes the same tests: same slot, same meaning, same register, same length give or take a word.

## How to use a group

1. Find the phrase in the email (case-insensitive, whole words).
2. Check the context rules below for that phrase. If a rule says no, skip it.
3. Keep the original as the first option, add the others, match capitalisation, pull in a preceding "a" or "an".
4. At 50 percent use 2 options, at 80 percent 3, at 100 percent up to 4 (never more than the group has).

### Contractions (always safe both ways)
I'm | I am. I'll | I will. I've | I have. you're | you are. you'll | you will. you've | you have. you'd be | you would be. you'd have | you would have. we're | we are. we'll | we will. we've | we have. they're | they are. don't | do not. doesn't | does not. didn't | did not. can't | cannot. won't | will not. isn't | is not. aren't | are not. wasn't | was not. haven't | have not. hasn't | has not. wouldn't | would not. couldn't | could not. it'd be | it would be. that's | that is. it's not | it is not. here's | here is. what's | what is.

Pick a direction that matches the email's register. A very casual email should not expand every contraction; a formal one should not contract every verb.

## Whole-sentence alternatives (for sentence and mixed modes)

Only use these when the sentence in the email means the same thing. Keep the original as option one.

- Call to action: Worth a quick chat? | Open to a short call? | Would a quick call make sense? | Up for a short conversation?
- Send offer: Mind if I send it over? | Want me to send it over? | Should I send it your way? | Okay if I pass it along?
- Interest check: Is this worth a look? | Does this sound relevant? | Worth exploring? | Is this on your radar?
- Opt-out: Not a fit? Reply no and I'll leave it there. | If this isn't relevant, say so and I'll stop. | Wrong person? Let me know and I won't follow up.
- Follow-up bump: Bumping this in case it got buried. | Following up on my last note. | Floating this back to the top of your inbox.
- Show how it works: Can I share how it works? | Want me to show you how it works? | Open to seeing how it works? | Mind if I walk you through how it works?
- Sign-off: Best, | Thanks, | Cheers, | Talk soon,

For any other sentence, build the rewrites from word-level swaps in that sentence (two or three swaps per rewrite) so the sentence structure and every fact stay the same.

## Context rules

These are the guards the engine applies. A model should apply them and go further when the sentence calls for it.

| Phrase | Rule |
|---|---|
| about, over | Only before a number or currency symbol. "about your team" and "over the line" stay. |
| get | Not before started, going, back, to, in, on, off, out, up, together, rid, it, done, this, that. |
| start | Not before with, by, off, up, over. |
| reach | Not before out or of. |
| talk | Only in "talk soon" or "talk to you soon". |
| simply | Not before put or said. |
| together | Not at the end of a sentence or before punctuation. |
| build | Only before a, an, the, your, our, their, this, that, list(s), system(s), campaign(s). |
| just | Only before a past-tense event (just opened, just raised, just hired, just launched, just moved, just won, just signed, just closed). Never "just wanted", "just following up", "just curious". |
| meetings, calls | Only right after book, booked, booking, set up, setting up, schedule, scheduled, more, held or a number ("booked 14 calls", "more meetings"). Anywhere else the words are not interchangeable. |
| meeting | Only right after a, first, quick, short, brief, intro. |
| call, chat, conversation | Only right after a, quick, short, brief, intro or "N-minute". "Answer every call" and "calls go to voicemail" stay. |
| already | Only inside a verb phrase ("have already been"). |
| email, note, message | Only when it refers to a specific email in this thread. Never in "cold email", "email list", "email address". |
| leads | Only when it means sales leads. "Finance leads" meaning finance leaders is not a swap. |
| growing | Not in "growing rapidly" or any headcount claim. |
| Capitalised words mid-sentence | Treat as proper nouns and leave alone (except "I" and sentence starts). |

## Never swap

Do not vary these, even at 100 percent.

- Merge tags, signature tags, links, email addresses.
- Every number, currency amount, percentage, date, time and duration: "14 calls" keeps 14 and keeps "calls" if it means phone calls.
- Product names, company names, people's names, place names, industries used as names (HVAC, SaaS), and anything in quotes.
- Meaning-changing pairs in context:
  - calls and meetings in "calls go to voicemail", "answer every call", "missed calls", "cold calls".
  - call and chat in "a sales call", "a discovery call" when the email means a specific type of call.
  - leads and prospects when "leads" means leaders ("finance leads", "team leads").
  - email and message in "cold email", "email deliverability", "email list".
  - growing and scaling in "growing rapidly", "growing team".
  - thanks for and I appreciate before a verb ("thanks for reading").
  - recently and just before "wanted", "following", "curious" or any non-event verb.
  - book and schedule when "book" means a book.
  - about and roughly before anything that is not a quantity.
  - a quick fix and a short fix: "quick" means easy here, "short" does not. Swap "quick" only with "easy" or "simple" in that slot, or leave it.
- Claims, proof points and offers: the thing being offered, the result quoted, the deadline, the price. Rephrase around them, never inside them.
- The subject line's variables and any number in the subject.
- Legal or compliance lines (unsubscribe wording the person's platform requires).

## Swaps that read wrong: never make these

The engine has context guards for these. A model must apply the same judgement.

- "cold message" or "cold note" for "cold email".
- "I appreciate reading" for "thanks for reading".
- "finance prospects" for "finance leads".
- "a short fix" or "a brief fix" for "a quick fix".
- "scaling rapidly" for "growing rapidly".
- Swapping "calls" to "meetings" in a sentence about phones.
- Expanding every contraction in a casual email so it reads stiff.
